# TheTroyGame — Creative Direction

Last reviewed: 2026-09-14

Canonical formula: **Trojan War × Cartoon Tower Defence × Adventure Comedy**.

The game is a bright, readable, exaggerated adventure built on recognizable Trojan War imagery. It is not a historical simulator, grim-dark military fiction, photorealism, or generic medieval fantasy.

## Tonal reference

Herc's Adventures is a tonal and readability reference only. Borrow high-level qualities such as playful adventure energy, exaggerated proportions, expressive acting, broad physical comedy, colorful environments, and immediate silhouette readability. Do not copy specific characters, costumes, poses, UI, environments, compositions, or assets.

## Tone rules

The game should feel heroic but playful, mythic but approachable, colorful, readable, expressive, and increasingly intense as Troy approaches its fall.

Humor should primarily come from staging and animation: oversized equipment, exaggerated anticipation, readable surprise/panic/triumph poses, physical hit reactions, expressive boss entrances, and small environmental gags that do not obstruct gameplay.

Avoid meme-heavy writing, default modern internet slang, gore-driven humor, and jokes that undermine every dramatic beat.

## Historical authenticity

Historical research defines the vocabulary, not the proportions. Weapons, armor, shields, architecture, ships, and faction motifs should remain recognizably Late Bronze Age / Mycenaean-inspired where practical, while scale, posing, shape language, and animation may be exaggerated for charm and readability.

A player should read **Trojan War** and **cartoon adventure** at the same time.

## Character acting

Use strong line-of-action poses, readable anticipation/action/recovery, controlled squash-and-stretch, exaggerated recoil/hit reactions, clear emotional states, and broad hero/boss gestures. Gameplay hit timing and telegraphs remain authoritative.

## Visual hierarchy

At gameplay zoom, read in this order: faction, role, threat level, current action/state, decorative detail.

## Campaign tonal arc

- Chapters I–II: bright, adventurous, energetic.
- Chapters III–IV: larger spectacle and stronger hero personalities.
- Chapter V: maximum siege spectacle; humor remains in reactions rather than reducing stakes.
- Chapter VI: deliberate tonal pause, suspicion, strange calm.
- Chapter VII: tragic climax with the same stylized language but less overt comedy and stronger emotional weight.

The fall of Troy remains serious in outcome even though the presentation is playful.

## Production rule

Any candidate that is technically correct but reads as realistic, grim, generic medieval fantasy, or visually solemn is off-direction and must not be promoted to `DONE`. Production-art acceptance requires consistency with this document and `MODEL_ART_INVENTORY.md`.
